OpenHamShack Radio {{VERSION}} preview

An all-band amateur transceiver for a Linux laptop: 160 m to 6 cm on a Pluto or a Hermes-Lite 2, with your UK licence class and the RSGB band plan built in.

The terrestrial sibling of OpenHamShack QO-100 NB (QO-100 narrowband), sharing its engine, and of OpenHamShack QO-100 WB (DATV, in a box). Back to openhamshack.com.

Status Preview

Preview — simulator-tested only; nothing tried on real radios or on air yet.

Every part of this release has been exercised against the program's own simulated bands, which is what it starts on. That proves the program hangs together; it says nothing yet about real hardware.

  • Pluto receive and transmit on 2 m and 70 cm are unproven: no Pluto has been connected to this program yet.
  • The Hermes-Lite 2 driver has been tested only against a software emulator of the radio, never a real one.
  • Power figures are estimates from numbers you type in (the Pluto's ~4.5 mW is one published measurement), not readings from a meter.
  • Transmit only within your own amateur licence. The program holds you to the class you set, but the licence condition is yours to keep — and nothing should reach an antenna until it has been checked into a dummy load and on a spectrum analyser.

Optional extras

Each one adds a feature; none is required to run the program, and the simulator needs none of them.

  • python3-libiio — the Pluto family: ADALM-Pluto, Pluto+, LibreSDR, Pluto nano
  • wsjtx — FT8 and FT4 (its jt9 does the decoding)
  • libcodec2 — FreeDV
  • python3-soapysdr — receive-only SDRs: RTL-SDR, Airspy, SDRplay-class

The Hermes-Lite 2 needs nothing extra: it is reached over your network.

What it does

  • Licence guard: Foundation, Intermediate or Full — every transmission is held inside what your class may use, at no more than its power there, per sub-allocation, from Ofcom's licence tables checked line by line against the published PDF. Bands your class does not have are refused with the reason.
  • Band-plan guard: the RSGB 2026 band plan is drawn on the scope and warns when a transmission leaves what its segment is for (or refuses, if you choose). Beacon and satellite sub-bands are always refused.
  • Power chain: per band, the radio's output, your amplifier's gain, feeder loss and antenna gain turn the drive control into an estimate of watts at the antenna; the drive cannot pass your licence's limit.
  • Modes: USB, LSB, CW, AM (transmit too), FM with CTCSS, repeater shift, reverse and the 1750 Hz toneburst; FT8 and FT4 at each band's dial; PSK31/63/125, RTTY, FreeDV and SSTV.
  • Band bar and band stacks: 160 m to 6 cm, three registers per band, VFO A/B, split, RIT and XIT.
  • Safety: a PTT sequencer for a PA (USB-serial RTS/DTR), SWR and PA-temperature trips where the radio measures them, a transmit time-out, dead-air and window watchdogs, and Esc always unkeys.
  • Logbook with ADIF export, memories seeded from the band plan, a second display, and a light theme.

Radios

  • Simulator (the default): busy simulated bands from 160 m to 6 cm with SSB, CW, FT8 and real UK repeater pairs that open to the right CTCSS tone. Nothing it does reaches the air.
  • ADALM-Pluto, Pluto+, LibreSDR or Pluto nano on 4 m (experimental), 2 m, 70 cm, 23 cm, 13 cm, 9 cm and 6 cm. A few milliwatts out and no output filter: its output is checked into a dummy load on a spectrum analyser before it drives any PA, and it never goes straight to an antenna. Untested on hardware.
  • Hermes-Lite 2 on 160 m to 10 m over Ethernet, about 5 W. Tested against an emulator only.
  • Receive-only SDRs through SoapySDR (RTL-SDR, Airspy, SDRplay-class).

6 m has no radio here yet: the Hermes-Lite 2 stops at 38.4 MHz and the Pluto cannot receive below 70 MHz.

Installing

  1. Download the .deb above and check it against the SHA-256 shown: sha256sum openhamshack-radio_*_all.deb.
  2. Install it with sudo apt install ./openhamshack-radio_<version>_all.deb — apt pulls in what it needs (python3-numpy, python3-pyqt6, python3-pyaudio) and the recommended extras from your normal repositories. It installs beside OpenHamShack QO-100 NB without touching it.
  3. Run OpenHamShack Radio from your applications menu, or openhamshack-radio from a terminal. It starts on the simulator, receiving; nothing transmits until you have ticked the licence box and keyed it yourself.

{{NOTES}}

Licence limits are Ofcom's (amateur licence conditions, 2024, checked against the PDF); the band plan is the RSGB's 2026 plan. FT8/FT4 decoding uses WSJT-X's jt9 (K1JT and the WSJT-X development group), FT8/FT4 encoding is OpenHamShack's own port of WSJT-X 2.7.0's (Joe Taylor K1JT, Steve Franke K9AN, Bill Somerville G4WJS et al.), and FreeDV uses libcodec2 (David Rowe, VK5DGR, and contributors).
