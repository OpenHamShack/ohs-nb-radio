OpenHamShack QO-100 NB {{VERSION}}

A QO-100 narrowband transceiver for a laptop or Raspberry Pi, with an ADALM-Pluto, LibreSDR or Pluto nano.

The sibling of OpenHamShack QO-100 WB (DATV, in a box) — this one runs on your own machine. Back to openhamshack.com.

Status Early preview

Proven end to end against the program's own simulated transponder: beacon lock, TX lock, and the FT8, CW and SSTV paths are all exercised automatically by the test suite against the simulator's modelled signals. Received on air: a tester's ADALM-Pluto, on Windows 11, locked to the middle beacon on the real QO-100 downlink. Not yet transmitted on air: TX lock and transmitting are proven only against the simulator, and no LibreSDR, Pluto nano or receive-only SDR has been tried yet. Raspberry Pi performance is a design estimate (the DSP budget it was written to), not a measurement on a Pi.

  • Transmit only within your own amateur radio licence, on the QO-100 narrowband uplink allocation.
  • Never above the beacon's level — the console's own guard helps, but the licence condition is yours to keep.

Optional extras

Each one adds a feature; none is required to run the program.
  • wsjtx — FT8 and FT4 (its jt9 does the decoding)
  • python3-libiio — the Pluto family: ADALM-Pluto, Pluto+, LibreSDR, Pluto nano
  • python3-soapysdr — receive-only SDRs: RTL-SDR, Airspy, SDRplay-class

What it does

  • Modes: USB/LSB voice; CW with a keyer and decoder; FT8 and FT4 (decoded by WSJT-X's jt9); PSK31, PSK63, PSK125; RTTY 45.45 and 170 baud; FreeDV 700C, 700D, 1600 and 2020 (via libcodec2); SSTV receive (Robot36, Martin M1, Scottie S1, PD120).
  • Beacon lock: tracks the middle 400 bd BPSK beacon or the lower CW F1A beacon and corrects the received frequency continuously, so signals sit where the bandplan says they should.
  • TX lock: finds your own transmission in your own downlink and corrects the uplink to match, so what you send lands where the VFO says — shown on the bandscope, not just claimed.
  • Beacon-level guard: measures your downlink against the beacon's level and can trim the power automatically to stay under it, on QO-100 NB's one hard etiquette rule.
  • Bandscope and waterfall across the whole transponder, with the bandplan strip, beacon markers, your own passband and where your signal actually landed.
  • Second display: an optional window for another screen — a large waterfall with decodes on it, or the mode panel, your choice.
  • Logbook, exported as ADIF, and memories for frequencies and modes you come back to.

Hardware

  • ADALM-Pluto, Pluto+, LibreSDR or Pluto nano for full duplex transmit and receive, via libiio — all AD936x-family devices under the same driver.
  • RTL-SDR, Airspy or an SDRplay-class receiver for receive only, via SoapySDR: useful for a second receiver, or for listening before you have a transmit-capable SDR.
  • An LNB with a 9750 MHz local oscillator (the common consumer PLL LNB) — the program measures and corrects for its drift against the beacons, rather than assuming it.
  • A Linux laptop (Debian, Ubuntu, Mint) or a Raspberry Pi 400/500 (Raspberry Pi OS). Nothing in the design is Linux-only except packaging.

Installing

  1. Download the .deb above and check it against the SHA-256 shown: sha256sum openhamshack-nb_*_all.deb.
  2. Install it with sudo apt install ./openhamshack-nb_<version>_all.deb — using apt install ./… rather than dpkg -i lets apt pull in the required packages (python3-numpy, python3-pyqt6, python3-pyaudio) from your normal repositories in the same step, and installs the Recommends (python3-libiio, wsjtx, libcodec2) by default.
  3. Add anything from Suggests yourself if you want it: soapysdr-module-rtlsdr or soapysdr-module-airspy for those receivers.
  4. Run OpenHamShack QO-100 NB from your applications menu, or openhamshack-nb from a terminal. It starts against the built-in simulator until you choose a real device in Settings, so there is always something to see and nothing transmits by accident.

A missing optional package disables just that one feature, with a sentence saying which package to install — it never stops the program.

Release notes

{{NOTES}}

QO-100 is Es'hail-2's narrowband transponder, operated under AMSAT-DL's bandplan and etiquette. Receive and decode use WSJT-X's jt9 (K1JT and the WSJT-X development group) for FT8/FT4 - whose encoding is OpenHamShack's own port of WSJT-X 2.7.0's (Joe Taylor K1JT, Steve Franke K9AN, Bill Somerville G4WJS et al.) - and libcodec2/FreeDV (David Rowe, VK5DGR, and contributors) for FreeDV. OpenHamShack QO-100 NB is not affiliated with AMSAT-DL, BATC or Analog Devices.
